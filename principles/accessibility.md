# Accessibility

Accessibility is structural. It is not a polish pass, optional setting, or compliance-only afterthought.

H.U.M.A.N. documents should describe who might be excluded, what alternate paths exist, and which obligations come from standards, platform guidance, law, user research, or product policy.

## Guidance

- Reference established standards instead of rewriting them as local folklore.
- Treat assistive technology as part of the interaction model.
- Include cognitive, motor, sensory, language, attention, and environmental constraints.
- Do not assume one modality can serve everyone.
- Do not let AI-generated accessibility replace semantic structure and real testing.

## Useful Questions

- Can the experience be completed without sight?
- Can it be completed without precise pointer control?
- Can it be completed without hearing?
- Can it be completed under stress, interruption, or low literacy?
- Are errors, state changes, and consequences perceivable?
- Does personalization preserve predictability?
